Advanced: what the sight scale was laid out for, and fitting

Each sight mark is modelled as the elevation the reference load needs at that range. Your firings then correct that model for your own rifle and load.

For each group, enter where its centre landed relative to where you wanted it. Up/down: + is high, − is low. Left/right (as you look at the target from the firing point): + is right, − is left. The ± buttons flip a number between the two, and the line under each box spells out which way it is. Or tap Plot shots and mark each hole on a picture of the target; the tool fills in the centre, size and shot count. In Sight set, put the mark or setting (e.g. 300 or 525) or B for the battle aperture, and any micrometer clicks in Clicks ±. Untick Use to leave out a flyer or a bad day.

Aim: Point of impact means you aimed at the spot you wanted to hit. Lollipop means you held the bottom of the chosen target (or of the round bull's black aiming mark) on top of the foresight. Either way, measure from the middle of the scoring circles; the tool allows for the hold-off.

Units apply to up/down, left/right and group size: in = inches at that range; MOA; ZR card in = inches on the Bisley Zero Range card (fired at 71′ 7″, where ¼ in ≈ 1 MOA). For Zero Range groups, put the card mark you aimed at (e.g. 300) as the range.

Trajectory

Height of the bullet above (+) or below (−) your line of sight, in inches, for the sight setting chosen. Tap or hover on the plot to read off any range. A different load is shown as if fired with the same sight setting on this rifle; treat comparisons as a guide until you have shot them.

How it works

A point-mass trajectory (G1 drag, your temperature and pressure) gives the elevation your load needs at every range. Each sight mark is treated as the elevation the reference load needs at that distance, which is how service sights were laid out.

Each test group tells the tool how much elevation that setting actually gave on your rifle. With groups at one setting it fits a fixed offset (in effect, foresight height). With groups at two or more settings 200+ yards apart it also fits a scale factor, which absorbs differences from the reference load. Residuals show how well the model explains each group; large ones point to a flyer, a typo or changed conditions.

Treat predictions beyond your furthest test range as a starting point and confirm on paper.
